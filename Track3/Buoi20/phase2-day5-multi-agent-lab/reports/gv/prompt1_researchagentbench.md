# ResearchAgentBench Design Document

## 1. Benchmark Goal
The primary goal of ResearchAgentBench is to evaluate AI systems designed to assist graduate students in completing research tasks. The benchmark aims to assess not only the fluency of generated text but also the usefulness, correctness, and research judgment of the AI's outputs in real-world academic scenarios.

## 2. Core Assumptions
- Graduate students require assistance in various stages of research, including literature review, data analysis, and writing.
- AI systems can provide meaningful support if they generate relevant, accurate, and contextually appropriate outputs.
- The benchmark will focus on practical tasks that reflect common challenges faced by graduate students.

## 3. Task Categories
1. **Literature Review**: Tasks related to finding, summarizing, and synthesizing academic literature.
2. **Data Analysis**: Tasks involving the interpretation and analysis of datasets.
3. **Writing Assistance**: Tasks focused on drafting, editing, and formatting academic papers.
4. **Research Design**: Tasks that require formulating hypotheses, designing experiments, or planning methodologies.
5. **Presentation Preparation**: Tasks related to creating presentations or visual aids for research findings.

## 4. Example Tasks
### Literature Review
1. **Task 1**: Identify and summarize five recent papers on a specified topic.
2. **Task 2**: Generate a synthesis of findings from three selected papers, highlighting gaps in the research.

### Data Analysis
3. **Task 3**: Analyze a provided dataset and generate a report on key trends and insights.
4. **Task 4**: Create visualizations (e.g., graphs, charts) that effectively communicate the results of the analysis.

### Writing Assistance
5. **Task 5**: Draft an abstract for a research paper based on provided findings and context.
6. **Task 6**: Edit a given draft for clarity, coherence, and adherence to academic standards.

### Research Design
7. **Task 7**: Propose a research question and outline a methodology for investigating it.
8. **Task 8**: Critique a provided research proposal, identifying strengths and weaknesses.

### Presentation Preparation
9. **Task 9**: Create a PowerPoint presentation summarizing a research project, including key findings and implications.
10. **Task 10**: Develop speaker notes for a 10-minute presentation based on a research paper.

### Adversarial Component
11. **Task 11**: Generate a fake but plausible research paper and ask the AI to identify flaws or misleading claims.
12. **Task 12**: Provide a poorly structured research question and evaluate the AI's ability to reformulate it into a coherent hypothesis.

## 5. Scoring Rubric
- **Usefulness (0-5 points)**: Relevance and applicability of the output to the task.
- **Correctness (0-5 points)**: Accuracy of the information provided.
- **Research Judgment (0-5 points)**: Ability to critically assess and synthesize information.
- **Overall Quality (0-5 points)**: Clarity, coherence, and adherence to academic standards.
- **Total Score**: 0-20 points per task.

## 6. Baselines to Compare Against
- **Human Baseline**: Outputs generated by experienced graduate students.
- **Existing AI Systems**: Performance of current AI models (e.g., GPT-4, BERT) on the same tasks.
- **Random Baseline**: Outputs generated randomly or through simple keyword matching.

## 7. Likely Failure Modes and Gaming Risks
- **Overfitting to Tasks**: AI systems may be trained to perform well on specific tasks without generalizing to broader research contexts.
- **Superficial Responses**: Systems may generate fluent but shallow content that lacks depth or critical analysis.
- **Gaming the Evaluation**: AI may produce outputs that are technically correct but lack genuine insight or originality, focusing on meeting rubric criteria rather than providing true assistance.

## 8. Human Evaluation Protocol
- A diverse panel of graduate students and faculty will evaluate AI outputs based on the scoring rubric.
- Each task will be rated independently by at least three evaluators to ensure reliability.
- Evaluators will provide qualitative feedback alongside quantitative scores to inform future iterations of the benchmark.

## 9. Limitations of the Benchmark
- The benchmark may not capture all nuances of research assistance, particularly in specialized fields.
- The reliance on human evaluators may introduce subjectivity and variability in scoring.
- The tasks may not fully represent the breadth of challenges faced by graduate students in different disciplines.

## 10. Recommendations for Version 2 of the Benchmark
- Expand task categories to include interdisciplinary research challenges.
- Incorporate more complex, multi-step tasks that require sustained engagement from the AI.
- Develop a more robust adversarial component to test the AI's ability to handle misinformation or poorly structured inputs.
- Explore automated scoring methods to complement human evaluations and reduce subjectivity.
